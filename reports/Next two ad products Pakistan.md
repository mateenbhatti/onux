# Back barcode scanners and projectors next

Run the next ads for two products priced above Rs 8,000 that can clear roughly Rs 2,700 profit per delivered parcel. The first is a **barcode scanner, bundled or paired with a label or POS printer**, which sells to the same shop-owner buyer as the SP-90A. The second is a **portable Android or 4K projector**, which has few local Pakistani advertisers. Backups are a **branded WiFi security camera** and a **receipt printer plus cash drawer bundle**. This is a ranking by price band and ad-market gaps, not by evidence of winners. No researcher found a single wholesale quote, and none could open a web page, query Google Trends, or reach TikTok Creative Center or Daraz rankings. The break-even arithmetic is the strongest part of the report. Using the seller's own numbers, the SP-90A nets about **Rs 1,012 per booking** (Rs 979 in the researchers' Rs 500 ad-cost version) and the Kisonli LP-2S nets about **Rs 55**, which is break-even. Any new product must therefore have roughly **Rs 2,700 or more profit per delivered parcel**, which means a wholesale cost under a ceiling you can compute for each retail price (table below). Both primaries are conditional: if the Hafeez Center quotes come in above that ceiling, drop them and move to the backups. The test costs about Rs 30,000, less than a third of the Rs 88,568 spent on TikTok.

## The seller's own formula sets a Rs 2,700 profit floor per delivered parcel

The researchers reverse-engineered a model that reproduces both of the seller's profit figures exactly. Profit per delivered parcel is (retail price + Rs 200 shipping) minus cost, minus Rs 500 courier, minus 4% of the order total. For the Kisonli: 3,800 − 2,000 − 500 − 152 = **Rs 1,148**. For the SP-90A: 12,700 − 9,000 − 500 − 508 = **Rs 2,692**. The Rs 500 courier and 4% tax components are the researchers' fitted assumptions, not figures the seller stated (computed in the margins note; the matching to both profit figures supports them). Profit per booking, meaning each order placed, is then 0.62 × delivered profit, less 0.38 × Rs 500 for returns (Rs 190), less the ad cost per booking.

At the seller's **Rs 467 per booking** Meta cost, the SP-90A earns 0.62 × 2,692 − 190 − 467 = **about Rs 1,012 per booking**. The LP-2S earns 0.62 × 1,148 − 190 − 467 = **about Rs 55**. That is why it should not get more ad budget. There is a catch. The seller also reports Rs 812 per purchase on Meta for printers. If that is the real cost per order that reaches the stage the formula counts, the SP-90A drops to **about Rs 667 per booking** and the LP-2S loses about Rs 290. The two figures use different definitions (booking versus purchase) and the seller should reconcile them in Ads Manager before trusting either. The TikTok spend of Rs 88,568 cannot be judged here because no TikTok results were provided.

A new product must clear a bar. To net Rs 1,000 per booking at a 38% return rate and Rs 467 ad cost, delivered profit must be (1,000 + 190 + 467) ÷ 0.62 = **about Rs 2,670**. At the pessimistic Rs 812 ad cost it must be about Rs 3,230. Rearranged, the wholesale cost ceiling for Rs 2,700 delivered profit is 0.96 × (price + 200) − 3,200. That gives these maximum costs:

| Retail price | Maximum wholesale cost | Cost as share of price |
|---|---|---|
| Rs 8,000 | Rs 4,672 | 58% |
| Rs 10,000 | Rs 6,592 | 66% |
| Rs 12,500 | Rs 8,992 | 72% |
| Rs 15,000 | Rs 11,392 | 76% |
| Rs 20,000 | Rs 16,192 | 81% |

(The SP-90A's Rs 9,000 cost sits just above the Rs 8,992 ceiling, which is why it earns about Rs 2,692.) Cheaper gadgets cannot reach this bar. Earbuds retail at Rs 899–1,349 and power banks at Rs 1,499–2,099 on Priceoye, so even a generous margin cannot cover a Rs 467 ad plus the courier fee and returns ([Priceoye earbuds](https://priceoye.pk/wireless-earbuds/assorted); [Priceoye power banks](https://priceoye.pk/power-banks)). The same arithmetic eliminates facial trimmers at Rs 1,299–1,999 and the wireless collar microphone unless it is sold in a bundle. Returns matter nearly as much as margin: the margins note estimates that dropping from 38% to 30% returns is worth roughly Rs 130–200 per booking.

## Scanners and projectors fill the gaps that ad data and price bands leave open

**Barcode scanners and label printers.** The product has a similar ticket to the SP-90A and sells to the same buyer. Retail snapshots found were Black Copper BC-8802 laser scanner at **Rs 15,180**, Zebra TLP 2844 label printer at Rs 23,000, and Deli E801P at Rs 14,300 ([W11stop scanner](https://w11stop.com/black-copper-printer-barcode-scanner-price-in-pakistan/black-copper-bc-8802-scanner-price-in-pakistan); [W11stop Zebra](https://w11stop.com/zebra-tlp-2844-label-printer); [W11stop Deli](https://w11stop.com/computing-and-gaming/pos-systems/barcode-scanner/deli-e801p-thermal-receipt-printer-price-in-pakistan)). Those are branded prices and some listings were sold out or had conflicting dates. A generic Hafeez Center scanner likely retails lower, so the real ceiling may be tighter than the table suggests. The expected math uses an illustrative, **assumed** cost: a Rs 15,000 scanner bought at Rs 11,000 gives delivered profit of 15,200 − 11,000 − 500 − 608 = **Rs 3,092**, or about **Rs 1,260 per booking** at Rs 467 ad cost and about Rs 915 at Rs 812 (computed from the margins note). The reason to prefer it is operational: the seller already has the creative style, page audience and printer customer list, and the Meta result for printers already exists. The researchers' argument that business buyers refuse less is only reasoning. One reseller guide argues the opposite, that a larger cash amount at the door makes the doorstep a decision point ([CODVerifier](https://hashnode.com/@codverifier)). The seller's own 38% return rate on a Rs 12,500 printer shows no sign of a high-ticket advantage so far.

**Portable Android or 4K projector.** The Meta Ad Library sample for "projector Pakistan COD" returned just **one** matching ad (PakBuy, "TV Price se kam mai Full Cinema Experience", started about Oct 17). A second local page, HW Projector Store, has run about 33 days, and a USD-billed projector ad has been active since about March 2026 ([Ad Library, PakBuy](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1679536103705704); [HW Projector Store](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1822237542354985); [USD-billed ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=917488587948410)). Compare this with earbuds, facial trimmers and collar microphones, each advertised by several Pakistani pages. The researchers could see neither spend nor impressions, so a thin field could equally mean a product that does not sell on COD. The retail price is not in the data, which is its biggest hole. If it sells near Rs 15,000, the cost ceiling is about Rs 11,400. The "TV price" hook fits Meta's video format, and a projector is a plausible wedding-season and winter-evening purchase, though that is inference, not data. The main risk is quality: cheap projectors that look worse than the ad video are the type of item where a buyer refuses at the door.

**Backups.** A branded WiFi camera retails at Rs 8,400–15,600 for EZVIZ models ([w11stop](https://w11stop.com/networking-security-surveillance/security-camera)). Markaz lists home security among growing categories, but that is a seller-platform marketing claim ([Markaz](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/state-of-ecommerce-pakistan-2026)). Cheap OLX clones at Rs 2,069–9,000 will drag the price down and raise refusals. The second backup is a **receipt printer plus cash drawer bundle**. A receipt printer alone fails on the numbers: a BC-5890-class printer at Rs 8,270 retail with an assumed Rs 6,200 cost earns only about Rs 230 per booking at Rs 467 ad cost, and loses money at Rs 812. Adding a drawer and paper can lift the order value, but the researchers found no clean drawer price (OLX bundles at Rs 5,500–25,000, [OLX](https://www.olx.com.pk/urdu-bazar_g16536/q-printer)).

**Products to skip.** Audio glasses (Rs 4,999–7,999, a single advertiser) would need roughly 40% gross margin, an unlikely wholesale spread, and lack evidence of demand. Heaters and geysers do have seasonal support from gas shortages ([IPS News](https://ipsnews.net/business/2024/12/24/what-are-the-reasons-for-the-growing-demand-for-electric-heaters-in-pakistan/)), but they sit outside the seller's range, are bulky, and carry fire-safety complaints; the researchers found no PSQCA standard source. Smartwatches face a 2026 customs valuation ruling that could change import costs ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/?p=1015460)). The seller already sells them, and the notes show the category is crowded.

## What is thin, unverified or missing

Every researcher reported that web pages could not be opened (DNS failures), so all findings come from search snippets, much of it from vendor and seller-platform blogs that promote their own services. These gaps change how far to trust the recommendation:

| Gap | Effect |
|---|---|
| **No wholesale prices** from Hafeez Center, Hall Road, Saddar or 1688 | The ceiling table cannot be checked. Neither primary is confirmed as profitable. |
| **No Google Trends data** (geo PK) | Seasonality for every product is unmeasured. |
| **No TikTok Creative Center, TikTok Shop PK or Daraz best-seller data** | No cross-channel confirmation of demand. The only Daraz category data found is from 2020. |
| **Meta Ad Library tool returns neither impressions, spend nor ad format**, and returns newest ads first | Long-running winners are under-sampled. The oldest confirmed PKR gadget ads were 33–57 days old. Ad counts are keyword matches, not product totals. |
| **No category-level COD return data**; only generic ranges (DHL about 18%, Simpaisa 25–30%, EasySell above 40%) | The 38% return rate cannot be benchmarked by category. |
| **No courier rate card** for Leopards or Trax | The Rs 500 courier fee is inferred from the seller's profit figures. |
| **No Pakistan-specific Q4 CPM data**; Pakistan CPM $0.60–1.80 is a third-party benchmark ([Stackmatix](https://stackmatix.com/blog/facebook-ads-cpm-by-country-2026)); US CPM roughly doubled from early November to the December peak in 2024 ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/holiday-cpms-2024/)) | Expect cost pressure around Nov 11 and Dec 11–18. Read your own Ads Manager history. |

Daraz is a further competitive threat. Its 11.11 sale in 2025 drew 48,000+ sellers beating prior-year sales, and its 12.12 sale ran Dec 11–18; both figures are company claims ([ProPakistani 11.11](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/04/darazmall-brands-lead-daraz-pakistans-11-11-sale-with-record-growth-and-customer-engagement-2/amp/); [ProPakistani 12.12](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/11/daraz-pakistan-closes-2025-with-12-12-grand-year-end-sale-and-one-last-wave-of-savings/amp/)). Buyers compare prices during these windows, so your price must hold up against marketplace discounts.

## A one-day validation checklist with pass and fail numbers

The numeric thresholds below are proposals built from the seller's formula, not benchmarks from the research. Run each against the four candidates: scanner, projector, WiFi camera, printer-plus-drawer bundle.

| Check | What to do | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hafeez Center quotes** (the deciding step) | Get written or WhatsApp quotes from at least 3 shops per product, with model, cost at 10 and 50 pieces, warranty, and whether returned stock can be exchanged. Ask for scanners (1D laser and 2D), label printers, the printer-plus-drawer bundle, 4K Android projectors, and 2MP–3MP WiFi cameras. | Lowest quote at or below the ceiling for your intended retail price (e.g. Rs 11,392 for Rs 15,000; Rs 4,672 for Rs 8,000), at least 50 pieces in stock, and a defect-exchange window of 7 days or more | Cheapest quote above the ceiling, or no exchange or warranty. Drop to the next candidate |
| **Daraz** | Search the product, sort by popularity and note price, sold count and reviews for the top 10 listings. Check whether a Daraz Mall or branded listing undercuts your planned price. | At least 3 listings with 50 or more reviews, median price of the top 10 at or above your planned retail, and the best branded listing no more than 10% below your price | Fewer than 3 listings with 50 reviews, or the branded listing 15% or more below your price. Recheck during 11.11 |
| **Meta Ad Library** (browse the site, not just the tool, so older ads appear) | Search the product name in Roman Urdu and English, country Pakistan, status active. Count distinct PKR-billed pages and ads running over 30 days. | 2–10 distinct Pakistani pages and at least 2 ads over 30 days (demand proven, room remains) | Zero advertisers with no Daraz demand, or more than 15 near-duplicate Pakistani pages (saturated) |
| **TikTok Creative Center** (Top Ads, Pakistan, last 30 days, Tech and Electronics) | Search the product keywords and note rank and views. | Product appears in Pakistan top ads with a demo-style video you can copy | Not found counts as neutral, not fail, because this source was never checked |
| **Google Trends** (geo Pakistan, 12 months and 5 years, Shopping and Web) | Compare "barcode scanner", "projector", "wifi camera" against "thermal printer" as the baseline | Last 4 weeks at least 50% of the thermal printer baseline and not falling versus the prior 3 months | Under 25% of baseline or falling sharply |
| **Your own data** | Pull return rate by SKU, and cost per booking and per purchase for printers by month. | Printer return rate of 38% or lower, with a clear reading on whether Rs 467 or Rs 812 is your real cost | Printer cost per purchase near Rs 812: set the pass bar for new products at D of Rs 3,230 or more |

A candidate must pass the Hafeez Center quote before it goes to the other checks. After that, it needs at least two of Daraz, Meta Ad Library and Trends, with no outright fail on Daraz.

## A Rs 30,000 test, scheduled before the 11.11 cost spike

Run the two best candidates that pass, with a cap of Rs 15,000 each over 7 days, starting about Oct 27 so most spend falls before the Nov 11 cost pressure. Daily budget is about Rs 2,000 per product. Use Meta only, with a COD offer, free delivery and an open-parcel line if the courier allows it (seen in Pakistani ads, [Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2102849903666509)), and phone or WhatsApp confirmation before dispatch, the lever that sources most consistently cite ([DHL](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-pk/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-best-practice/subscription-marketing/how-pakistani-e-commerce-sellers-can-reduce-cod-returns)). Test one demo-style video and one price-anchor image per product. Skip TikTok until Meta results exist, since the existing TikTok spend has no readout.

Scale only on delivered results, which arrive after the 7 days. Read the outcome around Nov 10, once couriers have settled most parcels. Use these rules:

| Metric | Pass | Kill or pause |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per booking | At or below Rs 600 (the maximum for Rs 1,000 per booking is 0.62 × D − 1,190; at D of Rs 3,000 that is Rs 670) | Above Rs 800 after 15 bookings, or Rs 3,000 spent with no booking |
| Confirmation rate | 75% or more of bookings confirmed by call | Under 60% |
| Delivered share | 65% or more (return rate 35% or lower) | Return rate above 45% |
| Realised profit per booking | Rs 800 or more (computed with actual costs) | Under Rs 300 |

With about 30 bookings per product, the return-rate reading is rough (roughly plus or minus 17 points), so treat a pass as permission to run a second Rs 15,000–30,000 round, not as proof. Do not stack a backup test on top unless a primary fails its quote check or the kill rule. Keep the Kisonli ads at maintenance level or off, since the formula puts them at break-even.

## Conclusion

The research does not name a proven winner. It narrows the field using the seller's own numbers: only products with a Rs 8,000+ retail price and a cost ceiling about 60–76% of price can match the printer, so the cheap gadgets that dominate Pakistani ads (earbuds, trimmers, collar microphones) are structurally unprofitable at Rs 467 per booking and 38% returns. Scanners follow from what already works, and projectors from a thin advertiser field, but each hinges on a quote nobody has yet. The day spent on Hafeez Center quotes decides more than all the ad research done here. If both fail, the right answer is to keep the printer ads running and put the next rupees into reducing returns, which the notes suggest is worth as much as a new product.
