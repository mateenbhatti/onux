# Stop paying Meta for parcels that come back

The biggest thing missing from your Meta ads is not an ad setting. It is any control over which orders are real before the parcel ships, plus any view of which products still make money after returns. Your tracking works (Purchase match quality is 9.3 out of 10) and Meta shows no errors and a 96/100 score, so the problem is not the pixel. Meta reports about **Rs 730-810 per purchase**, but Shopify shows that **58% of gross sales over the last 14 days (74% over 28 days) were reversed**, so a kept order costs roughly Rs 1,200-1,350 in ads if your 40% return figure is right, and more if Shopify's reversal figure is closer. The damage is concentrated: the Speed-X SP-90A printer took **55% of 30-day ad spend (Rs 57,047)** and about 92% of its orders reversed, yet two of the five ads in your new Rs 3,000/day campaign promote it. Ranked by money at stake and ease of fixing, the top three fixes are a confirmation step before dispatch, a per-product profit check, and a redesign of the new mixed campaign before it burns its first Rs 21,000 week. Be sceptical of anything promising a large jump: the account makes only about 30-35 purchases a week, so Meta's optimisation is working with very little data, and the published evidence for most fixes is vendor marketing, not independent testing.

## Returns, not ad cost, are the biggest leak and they vary hugely by product

Published Pakistan return rates are all vendor or agency numbers, clustering at about 18-35% for cash on delivery, with a DHL Pakistan page saying roughly one in five COD parcels returns ([DHL Pakistan](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-pk/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-best-practice/subscription-marketing/how-pakistani-e-commerce-sellers-can-reduce-cod-returns)) and a Shopify-focused guide citing 30-35% ([ACOD guide](https://acod-cod-docs.pages.dev/blog/cash-on-delivery-strategies-shopify-stores-pakistan)). The best hard figure is from Meesho's India prospectus: about **22-24% undelivered on COD versus about 3% prepaid** ([Base.blog](https://base.com/en-EN/blog/india-ecommerce-benchmarks-2026-how-brands-reduce-rto-in-india-and-hit-accuracy-sla-targets/)), while ClickPost's India network data shows 37.5% ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/ecommerce-rto-statistics-india)). Your 40% is at the top of every range, which means something structural is wrong, not that this is normal. Note that 40% is your own figure: Shopify cannot tell me returns from cancellations (it shows zero delivered and zero shipped orders), and its reversal percentage is a ceiling because it also counts cancellations and refunds.

Even so, the product split in your own Shopify and Meta data is the most useful thing in the audit. Measured over the last 14 days (my arithmetic on Shopify and Meta figures; sample sizes are 7-24 orders, so treat them as warnings, not verdicts):

| Product | Orders | Share of sales reversed | Meta cost per purchase | Ad cost per kept order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SP-90A thermal printer | 12 | 92% | Rs 1,704 | about Rs 21,000 |
| HP DeskJet 2320 | 24 | 72% | Rs 249 | about Rs 890 |
| BT600M portable printer | 8 | 45% | Rs 836 | about Rs 1,530 |
| WDR dashcam | 7 | 46% | Rs 467 | about Rs 865 |
| SP-320A cash drawer | 10 | 30% | Rs 445 | about Rs 640 |

The SP-90A spent Rs 20,453 in 14 days and kept only Rs 12,500 of sales, before paying for the product itself. The cash drawers, which are the heart of your new POS campaign, have the lowest reversal rates. The DeskJet is the opposite case: cheap to acquire but 17 of 24 orders reversed, and at the vendor estimate of Rs 500-700 per returned parcel ([ACOD guide](https://acod-cod-docs.pages.dev/blog/cash-on-delivery-strategies-shopify-stores-pakistan)) that is roughly Rs 8,500-12,000 in return shipping, more than the Rs 6,462 of ads. I do not know your product cost or courier rates, so I cannot say whether any of these products is profitable; that is itself gap number 2.

One more measured warning: Meta credits 72 purchases over 11-03 to 11-15 while Shopify shows 51 Facebook-referred orders in 14 days, so Meta's Rs 733 per purchase is about **Rs 1,035 per Shopify-confirmed Facebook order** before any returns. Meta counts views within one day and clicks within seven, which flatters it. Thirty of your 81 orders in this window have a blank or "meitshop" referrer, and nobody knows what they are (repeat customers, WhatsApp orders or admin-made orders are possible).

## Fixes 1 to 3: confirm orders, check each product's profit, redo the Rs 3,000 campaign

**Confirmation (rank 1).** Vendors agree on the pattern: send a WhatsApp message within minutes of the order asking the customer to confirm the address and the order, and phone anyone who does not reply within a couple of hours; do not dispatch until confirmed ([bot.space](https://www.bot.space/blog/how-to-reduce-rto-ecommerce), [Interakt](https://www.interakt.shop/whatsapp-business-api/reduce-rto-with-whatsapp-business-api-cod-confirmations/)). Claimed results range from 22-30% fewer returns for WhatsApp alone to 35-45% for WhatsApp plus a call ([HillTeck](https://hillteck.com/rto-reduction-flows.html)), and individual case studies show 18% falling to 14% or 24% to 14.4% ([Go4whatsup](https://go4whatsup.com/case-studies/saanvi-crafts/), [Ominiflow](https://ominiflow.com/blog/case-study-beauty-cod-rto)). All are self-reported by sellers of the tools. Expect a relative cut of 20-45% at best, and expect fewer orders shipped, because part of the gain is simply orders you stop sending. At 10-15 orders a day you can do this by hand for free: one person, one WhatsApp Business number, and a rule that an order unconfirmed after one day is cancelled. Judge it by kept orders per rupee of ads, not by return percentage. Also test a small advance payment on a subset of orders; one vendor anecdote says a Rs 50 deposit cut one store's returns from 35% to 12% ([EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/cod-partial-payment-deposit-reduce-rto-shopify-2026)), but no independent data exists and a larger advance will cost you orders. Bad addresses are named as about 18% of failed first attempts in Pakistan and Bangladesh in vendor-cited courier data ([EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/de-eu/blogs/wiki/cod-delivery-address-validation-shopify-fix-2026)), so the confirmation message should repeat the full address back.

**Product check (rank 2).** Build one sheet with a row per product: selling price, what you pay the supplier, courier cost out and back, and the share of orders that actually came back, taken from your courier reports (the one number I could not get). The break-even ad cost per placed order is (1 minus return rate) times your margin after outbound courier, minus the return rate times the courier cost of both legs. At a 40% return rate every ad rupee is worth only 60 paise before courier costs. Pause any product whose ad cost per kept order exceeds that figure. On the evidence above, take SP-90A out of the new campaign until you know why 11 of 12 orders reversed (wrong price on the page, a bad supplier, or ad traffic that buys on impulse).

**The new Rs 3,000/day campaign (rank 3).** It launched on 11-16 with one ad set and five ads covering cash drawers, the BT600M printer and the SP-90A, and had zero spend when I pulled the data, so there is no performance to judge yet. Meta says an ad set usually needs about 50 optimisation events a week to exit learning ([Meta help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/910877842876429)). At your recent Rs 730-810 per purchase, Rs 3,000/day buys about 26-29 purchases a week, so it will probably stay under that line, and with three prices in one ad set Meta will favour whichever product produces cheap orders fastest, not the one you profit from. The practical-minded fix is to run drawers (and the printers that go with them) as a shopkeeper-focused set without the SP-90A, keep the dashcam separate, and not touch the campaign for 3-5 days; vendor guidance says to spend two to three times your target cost per result before judging an ad ([Flighted](https://www.flighted.co/blog/how-to-know-when-to-pause-a-meta-ad)). Starting at Rs 3,000 is five times your best-performing test budget, so consider starting at Rs 1,500 and raising by about 20% steps every 3-4 days, a common practitioner rule rather than a Meta one ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/facebook-ads/how-to-scale-facebook-ads/)).

## Fixes 4 to 6: pages, abandoned checkouts, and what you tell Meta

**Product pages (rank 4).** Only 4.5% of sessions add to cart, and 95% of sessions are on mobile (Shopify). Facebook traffic converts at 1.76% versus 4.8% for direct, but direct visitors are warmer, so that gap overstates the problem. The pages themselves are measurably thin: the DeskJet, your top order driver, has 2 images, no video and a description of about 900 characters against 2,800-4,100 for the others; only 1 of 7 advertised products has a video; reviews exist on only 1 of 7; and open-parcel is mentioned on only one page. Ads land in a store of 1,644 products. For each advertised product, add the ad's own video, real customer photos, your Hafeez Center shop address and a WhatsApp button near the top, and state the delivery time and whether you allow opening the parcel (confirm your courier supports it first; policies vary, and Priceoye charges Rs 300 for it, per [Priceoye](https://priceoye.pk/parcel-policy)). Nobody has measured the lift from these changes in Pakistan, and I did not test page speed, so measure the add-to-cart rate for a week before and after.

**Abandoned checkouts (rank 5).** Shopify lists 12 abandoned checkouts with contact details in 14 days, worth Rs 285,550, including Rs 84,000 of cash drawer carts and repeated thermal roll plus SP-90A carts. Call them the same day. This costs nothing, uses the same script as confirmation, and vendors claim 14-25% of carts can be recovered ([Go4WhatsUp](https://www.go4whatsup.com/guides/whatsapp-for-ecommerce/index.html)), though they will carry the same return risk. A Meta retargeting ad set is not worth it: with 156 carts in 14 days the audience is too small.

**What Meta is told (rank 6).** Every Meta campaign optimises on standard Purchase, which fires when the order is placed, so Meta cannot tell a kept order from a refused one; a local agency blog makes the same point ([WePromS](https://weproms-pk.pages.dev/blog/meta-ads-automation-pakistan-sme-budget-trap/)). The fix vendors describe is to send Purchase only after confirmation, via the server connection, with the same event ID as the browser copy ([eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/facebook-conversions-api-shopify-cod-2026)). I would not do this yet. With about 33 purchases a week, filtering out a further share leaves Meta even less data, and Meta says custom events cannot be used for optimisation, so the confirmed stage has to use a standard name ([Meta help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/3182661985172509)). First run confirmation for a month; then decide. What you can do today is fix the lists: the four audiences created on 2026-10-26 are unused by any ad set, and the "Purchasers 180d" audience is explicitly labelled as including returned orders. Rebuild the customer list from delivered orders only, make a separate list of people who refused parcels, and exclude that second list from your campaigns.

## What is measured, what is claimed, and what is unknown

Measured in your accounts: the campaign structure, budgets, cost per purchase by campaign, frequency of 1.5 on active campaigns (not worn out), tracking health, the funnel numbers, the reversal figures, the page contents, and that no audience or exclusion is used. Claimed only by vendors: every effect size for confirmation, deposits, WhatsApp recovery and advance payment; the 18-35% Pakistan return range; Rs 500-700 per return; and the creative rules about hooks and concept counts. Inferred by me, not measured: that Audience Network and Threads placements produce low-quality orders (no placement breakdown was pulled), that the "UGC facts" style beats "problem" style (Rs 220 versus Rs 715 for the dashcam, Rs 347 versus Rs 741 for the SP-320A, from only 3-9 purchases per ad), and that deduplication works (inferred from counts; I could not read event IDs). Unknown: your product costs and courier rates, the true return rate by product and courier, why 30 orders have no referrer, and whether the ad sets are in learning-limited status (the tool did not return it).

Three items I deliberately ranked low. Meta suggests merging the Dashcam and SP-320A ad sets; I would ignore that, because those buyers (car owners and shopkeepers) are different people, and the real reason the Rs 600-800/day ad sets are weak is budget, which merging unrelated products does not fix. Meta's 96/100 score ignores returns entirely and should not reassure you. Turning on automatic advanced matching, fixing the catalog (it fails one required check, so dynamic ads would be unreliable) and trying a WhatsApp lead campaign for POS buyers are all low-value or unproven: I found no independent evidence on targeting shopkeepers with Meta ads in Pakistan.

## Conclusion

The sceptical reading of this account is that the ads are not obviously the problem: Rs 52,776 of 14-day ad spend sits against Rs 521,840 of net sales, though that ratio says nothing about profit without your product costs. What looks wrong is that every decision, by you and by Meta, is made on placed orders, which are only about 40-60% real. Fixing that means one human confirming orders and one spreadsheet of per-product profit, not a new tool or a new campaign structure.

The new campaign is the main near-term risk, because it multiplies spend on an unproven mix, including a product whose recent orders almost all came back. Do the cheap things first (rank 1, 2, 3, 5 this week), measure kept orders per rupee for a month, and only then touch tracking and audiences. If kept-order cost does not fall after confirmation is running, the problem is the products or the courier, not Meta.
