# Scale the WDR dashcam ads in small steps

Scale, but slowly, and treat the ads as the safest part of the plan: the weak points are returns, stock cash and one policy rule, not the Rs 600 campaign itself. On the numbers pulled on 17 Nov, the campaign spent **Rs 3,498 for 10 Meta-credited purchases (Rs 350 each)** in 7 days, and on the shop's own figures an order can absorb **Rs 800-1,500 of ad cost** before profit hits zero (Rs 1,054 at 40% returns, Rs 828 at 50%). So even if scaling doubles your cost per purchase, the ad side still pays; what can actually hurt you is returns, which nobody has measured yet. Be sceptical of the Rs 350: it rests on 10 purchases, daily cost swung from Rs 54 to Rs 801, and only 5 of 11 Shopify dashcam orders are credited to Facebook (Rs 700 per Facebook-credited order), so plan on a true cost of **Rs 350-700**. The recommended path is: change nothing in Meta for about 4 days while you set up order confirmation and check the creative for policy risk; then add two genuinely different ads (not a budget change); then raise the budget about 20-25% at a time, no faster than every 4 days, from Rs 600 toward Rs 1,100 over about 4 weeks, and only toward Rs 1,400-1,800 if returns, stock and cost all pass their gates. Every number in the ladder is my judgement from practitioner rules of thumb. No published or measured evidence exists for how a Rs 600/day Pakistan COD dashcam campaign responds to budget increases, and this report says so wherever that matters.

## What your 7 days of data can and cannot tell you

The data below are live pulls from your Meta ad account and Shopify store (no web source; the figures are yours). Your 7-day Meta window shows **9,474 impressions, 337 link clicks and 10 purchases**, a click-to-purchase rate of 3.0%, with frequency of only 1.5 over the week, so there is no sign of audience fatigue yet. Daily cost per purchase ranged from Rs 54 (a partial day, 3 purchases) to Rs 801 (1 purchase), and one day (Rs 687 spent) had zero purchases. Moving one purchase on a three-purchase day changes that day's cost by more than Rs 100. Excluding today's partial day it is Rs 477 (7 purchases for Rs 3,336), so the honest range for "Meta's CPA" is about Rs 300-480, not a point value. Cost per thousand impressions fell from Rs 400-500 to about Rs 310-330, which suggests delivery is getting cheaper, but purchase counts did not follow, so this is not proof of improvement.

Shopify shows **11 dashcam orders and 17 units** in the same window, Rs 102,000 gross and Rs 36,000 reversed, but all of that reversal is one order of 7 units from Dera Murad Jamali where 6 units were reversed. The other 10 orders have no reversals yet, but parcels are still in transit, so this is not a return rate. Treat the 7-unit order as a warning sign about bulk or fake orders, not as a statistic. Shopify credits only 5 of the 11 orders to Facebook (6 are blank or "meitshop" referrers), and Meta counts views within one day and clicks within seven, so Meta's count and your real order count will never match exactly. The earlier audit of your account found the same pattern: Meta reported about Rs 733 per purchase but about Rs 1,035 per Shopify-confirmed Facebook order, and 30 of 81 orders had no referrer, so some Meta-versus-Shopify gap is normal. The same audit, over 14 days, showed 7 dashcam orders with 46% of sales reversed. That earlier sample is too small to call and covers a different window, so it is a warning, not a measurement.

What is measured and what is not: the ad-set facts (one Advantage+ audience, Pakistan-wide, purchase-optimised, all placements allowed) and the daily numbers are measured. The tool did not return the learning-phase status, bid strategy or daily budget, so I cannot confirm any of these from data. No audience-size estimate, no purchase-by-region figure from Meta, and no courier delivery status exist in any of the data. At about 10 purchases a week the ad set is far below the roughly 50 conversions a week that vendors say an ad set needs to exit the learning phase ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-learning-phase-guide), [Hightouch](https://hightouch.com/blog/the-learning-phase)). Reaching that at Rs 350-480 per purchase would take roughly Rs 2,500-3,400 a day, which is my arithmetic, and it is not a realistic near-term target. Do not scale to chase it.

## Fix the 69% spend skew by adding ads, not by pausing the "winner"

Meta gave **69% of spend (Rs 2,421) to the "UGC problem" ad and 31% (Rs 1,077) to "UGC facts"**, and the two ads have the same number of purchases (5 each). The cost difference is real at the top of the funnel: facts had a click-through rate of 5.88% against 3.90%, Rs 6.12 per click against Rs 15.04, and Rs 285 per thousand views against Rs 425. Cost per purchase (Rs 215 against Rs 484) is not distinguishable, because 5 purchases each is far too few, and per click the "problem" ad converted slightly better (5 of 161 clicks against 5 of 176). The sensible reading is that facts buys attention cheaper, and nothing yet proves it sells better. About 500 people saw both ads. Meta's allocation follows its own predictions and cannot be forced from the budget screen, and I found no source that tells you whether pausing an ad inside a running ad set resets learning. Vendor guidance lists changes to creatives as a possible learning trigger, but none of it is Meta documentation.

The practical way through is to leave both ads running, add two new ads built on the facts approach but showing different ideas, and pause "problem" only when the new ads have had time. Practitioner sources agree that variety of idea beats volume of near-copies, and that Meta's own engineering post says nothing about how many ads to run ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/), [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda-creative-diversification/)). Creative counts in the 15-30 range quoted by agencies are for accounts spending thousands of dollars a month and are not evidence for you ([Pipeboard](https://pipeboard.co/guides/creative-volume-andromeda)). With Rs 600 a day, 4-5 live ads in total is enough. Sources also disagree on whether new ads belong in the existing ad set (Loomer) or a separate one (Segwise) ([Loomer](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/do-you-need-separate-ad-sets-for-customer-personas/), [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/where-to-test-new-creatives-meta-ads.md)), and nobody measured it. I recommend the existing ad set, because a second ad set would split your roughly 10 weekly purchases in two, and I flag that as my judgement. Ideas worth testing, all mine and untested: an overnight or low-light recording demo, a parking-monitor demo, a "what to do after an accident, proof for the insurer or police" angle, and a COD plus open-parcel trust ad. Make at least one in 9:16 vertical, since Reels delivered 59% of spend at Rs 295 per purchase (7 purchases) against Feed's 40% at Rs 463 (3 purchases). That gap is directional only.

Leave placements, ages and regions alone for now. Instagram got zero spend and Audience Network about Rs 2, so there is nothing to cut. Men 35-44 had the worst cost (Rs 592) and 55-64 the best (Rs 132), but those are 2 purchases each, and narrowing an audience that Meta is still learning would shrink delivery to save nothing. The hour pattern (about 7 of 10 purchases between 13:00 and 19:00 Pakistan time, almost none from 02:00 to 06:00) is also from 10 purchases; do not set dayparting. The one real use is operational: orders landing in the evening and at night will wait until morning for confirmation.

## The scaling ladder, with waits, gates and revert rules

Budget raises are the lowest-risk move at this scale. Twenty percent of Rs 600 is Rs 120, and the usual rule that changes above about 20% reset learning is practitioner folklore, with no Meta page confirming it ([Cometly](https://www.cometly.com/post/facebook-ads-learning-phase-optimization), [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/learning-phase-optimization)); Jon Loomer argues Meta now tolerates larger raises ([Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/updates-to-meta-ads-budgeting/)). I use 20-25% steps anyway because they cost almost nothing and keep each change attributable. Do not change the budget and add creative on the same day, since you could not tell which one moved the result ([Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/scaling-strategies)). The earlier audit already suggested Rs 1,500 start and 20% steps every 3-4 days for your new Rs 3,000 campaign; the same logic applies here, from a lower base.

| Step | When (from today) | Daily budget | What changes | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Days 0-3 | Rs 600, no edits | Set up confirmation, courier returns report, policy check, make 2 new ads | Confirmation process running; reorder lead time known |
| 1 | Day 4 | Rs 600 | Add the 2 new ads to the existing ad set | Hold 5 days; no budget change |
| 2 | Day 9-10 | Rs 720 (+20%) | Budget only; pause "problem" ad if its cost per click is still above 1.5x the others | 7-day Meta cost at or below Rs 700, 5 or more purchases since the raise |
| 3 | At least 4 days later | Rs 900 (+25%) | Budget only | Same, plus first delivered and returned results by order cohort |
| 4 | At least 4 days later | Rs 1,100 (+22%) | Budget only | Same, plus stock cover of at least 21 days |
| 5 | At least 7 days later | Rs 1,400 | Budget only; consider a new idea ad | All gates, return rate measured and below 40% |
| 6 | At least 7 days later | Rs 1,800 | Budget only | Same; this is the practical ceiling until stock and cash are re-planned |

Reaching Rs 1,100 takes about 4 weeks and Rs 1,800 about 6-7 weeks; I picked these by judgement from the practitioner pacing advice of 10-20% every 3-4 days ([Cometly](https://www.cometly.com/post/facebook-ads-learning-phase-optimization), [Leadenforce](https://leadenforce.com/blog/scaling-facebook-ads-from-5-to-500day-without-killing-performance)) and slowed to fit your tiny sample. Judge every step on a 7-day rolling total, never one day: your daily cost went from Rs 54 to Rs 801 with no change in settings. Do not duplicate the ad set or launch a new campaign for the same product; duplication splits your few purchases, creates overlapping auctions with yourself, and no source quantifies the benefit, only that practitioners argue about it ([Leadenforce](https://leadenforce.com/blog/when-duplicating-campaigns-works-better-than-increasing-budget)). Do not switch to cost caps or minimum-ROAS goals: with roughly 10 purchases a week there is no history to calibrate against and a cap below the real price starves delivery ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/cost-cap-vs-bid-cap-facebook-ads)).

Revert and stop rules, all my judgement, designed to ignore single-day noise: drop back one step (not pause) if the 7-day Meta cost per purchase exceeds **Rs 700** after at least 8 purchases, since that is double your baseline and close to the Rs 828 break-even at 50% returns. If any step spends **Rs 1,400-1,500 with zero purchases**, drop back one step; sources suggest 1.5-2x target cost per result ([AnyTrack](https://anytrack.io/blog/meta-ads-automation-rules-that-actually-work-in-2025)), but your own day with Rs 687 spent and no purchase shows that threshold would trigger on normal noise. Treat frequency above 2.5 with click-through falling more than 15% over 7 days as saturation, and then add creative instead of budget; those thresholds are vendor heuristics with no Meta source ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/creative-fatigue.md), [Sociallyin](https://sociallyin.com/resources/paid-social-ad-fatigue/)). Never make an edit and a revert within 3 days of each other. If an automated rule is used, pair it with a minimum-spend condition, and set your own calendar reminder as well. Pausing for more than 7 days is said to reset learning (vendor claim), so prefer stepping down to pausing.

## Margin, returns and cash decide how far to go, not Meta

Using your inputs (price Rs 6,000, Rs 200 delivery paid by the customer, supplier cost Rs 3,696, Leopards freight about Rs 300 and 96% COD remittance), a delivered order nets about **Rs 1,956 before ads**. The ad cost you can afford per placed order falls with returns: about Rs 1,505 at 20% returns, Rs 1,279 at 30%, Rs 1,054 at 40% and Rs 828 at 50%, assuming a returned parcel costs only the Rs 300 outbound freight and the stock is resellable undamaged (assumptions, not measurements). At a cost of Rs 350 you stay profitable up to about 71% returns; at Rs 700 up to about 50%. Two things could make this too rosy. First, the Finance Act 2025-26 made couriers withhold 2% income tax plus 2% sales tax on COD, which is about Rs 240 per Rs 6,000 parcel on my arithmetic if both apply to the full amount, and your freight "16% tax" may or may not already include it ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/07/02/courier-companies-announce-increase-in-delivery-charges-after-new-taxes/amp/), [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40370763)). If it is extra, subtract Rs 240 and your break-even at 40% returns is nearer Rs 900. Second, if returns cost the round trip, not one way, break-even at 40% falls to Rs 934. Confirm both with your accountant and Leopards statements before you pass Rs 900 a day.

Returns are the unmeasured ceiling. Pakistan figures of 18-40% are vendor or marketing pages (a DHL Pakistan page about 18%, other vendors 25-30% and "above 40%") and the best harder number I found is Indian: Meesho's prospectus implies about 22-24% undelivered on COD ([DHL Pakistan](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-pk/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-best-practice/subscription-marketing/how-pakistani-e-commerce-sellers-can-reduce-cod-returns), [Simpaisa](https://simpaisa-website.pages.dev/blogs/why-cash-on-delivery-is-killing-your-e-commerce-business-and-what-to-do), [Base](https://base.com/en-EN/blog/india-ecommerce-benchmarks-2026-how-brands-reduce-rto-in-india-and-hit-accuracy-sla-targets/)). No source measures how Pakistani returns change when ad spend rises; the mechanism (colder audiences, rushed handling) is plausible and unmeasured, so treat it as a risk to watch, not a fact ([eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/facebook-ads-cod-mena-testing-scaling-2026)). So the return gate works as follows: read delivered versus returned by order day for orders at least 10-14 days old, before each raise from Step 3. Pause raises if returns are above 40% and drop back to Rs 600 if above 50%.

Confirmation is the cheapest safeguard. Vendors claim WhatsApp confirmation cuts returns 22-30% and WhatsApp plus a call 35-45%, and single case studies show 24% falling to 14.4% ([HillTeck](https://www.hillteck.com/blog/whatsapp-api-vs-ivr-rto-reduction.html), [Ominiflow](https://ominiflow.com/blog/case-study-beauty-cod-rto)); all are from sellers of the tools, and part of any gain is simply orders you no longer send. Expect a modest effect. At 2-5 orders a day it is a free manual job: WhatsApp message within minutes repeating the full address, a call if unanswered after a couple of hours, and cancellation if unconfirmed after a day. Add one rule for this product: call every order of 2 or more units and every order from a remote city before dispatch, since the one outlier order was 7 units to a remote town. Measure success in kept orders per rupee of ad spend, not return percentage. Cost per delivered-and-collected order is the real metric ([eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/facebook-ads-cod-mena-testing-scaling-2026)). Do not change what Meta optimises on (confirmed-only purchase events) at 10 purchases a week; the earlier audit reached the same view.

Stock and cash set a hard ceiling. Shopify shows **89 units available** (4 committed) at Hafeez Center, which is about Rs 329,000 of stock at Rs 3,696 each. At today's 1.5 orders a day that is about 2 months; if Rs 1,100-1,800 a day truly produced 3-4.5 orders a day (a linear guess that probably overstates it), cover drops to 20-30 days, before returns come back. Supplier lead time and minimum order are unknown and nobody found them, so reorder when cover falls below your lead time plus 10 days. Cash is the second limit: couriers reportedly hold COD cash 7-15 days, with PostEx reported to advance cash for a fee ([Simpaisa](https://simpaisa-website.pages.dev/blogs/why-cash-on-delivery-is-killing-your-e-commerce-business-and-what-to-do), [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2021/10/05/pakistans-fintech-postex-raises-1-5mn-for-its-cod-financing-and-logistics-platform)). My illustrative arithmetic: 3 orders a day at about Rs 4,450 (stock, ads and freight) held for about 17 days ties up roughly Rs 230,000, against about Rs 115,000 at today's pace. Check that number against your real remittance days before Step 4. Also note that your Rs 6,000 sits at the top of marketplace listings for the same unit (Rs 4,600-6,150 on Mustafa Computers and Laptab; the Rs 4,600-4,700 listing is a sale price), so more reach means more shoppers comparing with Daraz ([Mustafa Computers](https://mustafacomputers.pk/products/wdr-dashcam-3-camera-lens-video-car-dvr-full-hd-1080p-triple-camera-night-vision-parking-monitor), [Laptab](https://www.laptab.com.pk/wdr-dashcam-3-camera-lens.html)). I found no demand ceiling data, so I cannot say at what spend the audience of car owners runs out.

## Policy risk: the interior camera is the one thing that can stop everything

Meta's Advertising Standards prohibit ads promoting spy cameras, phone trackers and other hidden surveillance equipment ([Meta Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/cheating-and-deceitful-practices/)); I could only read a search snippet, not the full policy, and no source says whether openly sold dashcams are treated the same way. The 3-camera unit includes an interior lens, which makes the framing risky. I found no enforcement cases for dashcams. Keep every ad about accidents, road evidence and parking; show the device visibly mounted; avoid words like "hidden", "secret", "spy", "catch", "watch your driver or staff" and any shot that implies recording people without their knowledge. Review your four existing ads and the landing page for those words today. Disapproval risk is a single point of failure because one product and one ad account carry the campaign (my inference). Before going past Rs 900, keep a second ad account or business manager ready if you can, and keep the original ads unedited so a disapproval of a new ad does not take down the winner. Whether higher spend draws more policy review is unknown.

## Conclusion

The owner's fear is partly right and partly aimed at the wrong target. A budget raise of Rs 120 will not break a campaign, and with a break-even of Rs 800-1,500 against an observed cost of Rs 350-700, the ad side has room for cost to double. The real exposure is that the only figure that decides profit, the share of parcels that come back, does not exist yet, and every raise adds parcels whose outcome arrives about two weeks later. That timing argues for a ladder whose speed is set by delivered-order data, not by Meta's dashboard, which is why the plan pairs each raise with a returns gate.

The most useful first moves cost almost no ad money: a confirmation routine, a courier return report by city, a policy check of the creative, and two new ads that fix the 69% allocation problem without touching the winner. If after about 4 weeks the Rs 1,100 level holds a 7-day cost under Rs 700 and returns are under 40%, you will have a measured basis, for the first time, to decide whether to go further. If not, stepping back to Rs 600 costs you nothing but time.
