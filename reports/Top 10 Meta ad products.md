# Back three proven products, test seven bets

**Advertise the Speed-X SP-90A receipt printer first, the HP DeskJet 2320 second, and treat the other eight picks as bets whose cost prices the owner must supply before any rupee is spent.** Only three of the ten picks have real sales proof in the Meit Shop data (SP-90A: 127 orders, 13% cancelled; Kisonli LP-2S: 79 orders, 13% cancelled; DeskJet 2320: 31 orders, 23% cancelled). Of the three, only the SP-90A and DeskJet have both volume and confirmed margin (Rs 3,580 and Rs 5,200 gross profit per order, using the owner's cost prices). The LP-2S sells but its cost is unknown, and the earlier assumption of about Rs 2,000 put it at break-even. Seven picks (BT600M portable printer, barcode scanners, a printer-plus-cash-drawer bundle, label printers, game sticks, a cash counting machine, one visible WiFi camera) rest on 1 to 5 orders each plus thin market evidence. Of these, only the BT600M has a known cost; for the other six this report states the maximum cost that still clears the profit bar rather than guessing a cost. The store lists 1,626 active products but only about 44 have ever sold, 4 products carry the revenue, and the sales history cannot separate ad-driven from organic demand, so "proven" means "sold while ads were running," not "proven by ads." The Meta policy points come from search summaries only, and no Meta policy page could be opened. Given Rs 1,000 per day style budgets and the Daraz 11.11 / 12.12 window, the practical plan is to protect the SP-90A, test the DeskJet with phone confirmation, and release one bet at a time only after its cost clears the ceiling given below.

## How strong is the evidence behind each pick

The ranking uses three kinds of evidence, and they carry very different weight. The strongest is the store's own Shopify order data, pulled on 6 Nov 2026 for 300 orders placed between 13 Sep and 6 Nov. It shows what sold and what was cancelled before dispatch (58 orders, 19.3%, mostly voided before payment). It does not show delivered or returned-to-origin parcels, because Shopify has no such status, so the true return rate is unknown; the 142 "fulfilled, payment pending" orders are likely COD parcels whose cash is not yet recorded. The earlier report cited a seller-reported 38% return rate on printers, which is the only return figure for this store and is not visible in the Shopify fields. The second layer is owner-supplied cost prices, which exist for only seven items (SP-90A Rs 9,120; BT600M Rs 8,304; DeskJet 2320 Rs 15,000; M111W Rs 34,000; thermal roll Rs 200; JOYROOM EC06 Rs 1,150; Kisonli K12 Rs 4,944). The third layer is market evidence: an Ad Library snapshot of Pakistan-active ads on 6 Nov 2026, which returns page names, titles and start dates but no spend, impressions, format or ad text, and category notes drawn from search summaries and seller blogs, which carry commercial interest.

Two structural caveats shape every pick. First, the store's catalog is the opposite of its sales: mobile accessories (219 listings), keyboards and mice (123) and power banks (86) have the most listings but near-zero orders, while printers, scanners and POS gear, where the store has a distinctive wholesale position, generate roughly 61% of non-cancelled revenue (Rs 1.55m of Rs 2.54m). Because Meta ads have mostly pushed printers and the Kisonli speaker, those orders reflect ad effort as much as organic category demand, and any product the store has not advertised has had no fair chance to show what it can sell. Second, margin arithmetic here uses a simple model: profit per order placed equals delivered share times (price minus cost minus Rs 500 courier), minus the courier cost on failed parcels, minus the Meta cost per placed order. It leaves out the Rs 200 shipping income and the 4% deduction the earlier report assumed, and both the Rs 500 courier and the Rs 800 cost per purchase are owner-supplied figures that no Pakistani rate card or benchmark confirmed. The floor that emerges is about **Rs 2,000 gross profit per order placed to break even at Rs 800 per purchase, and Rs 2,800 or more to earn a real return**; Rs 1,710 earbuds, with Rs 560 of gross profit, lose money at any delivery rate.

## The two printers are the only picks with proof and margin

**1. SP-90A (and its roll add-on).** The SP-90A is the store's one proven product by every measure: **151 units, 127 orders, Rs 1.42m of non-cancelled revenue, 13% cancelled, 105 fulfilled orders**, realised average price Rs 12,476 (Meit Shop Shopify data). At the owner's cost it earns Rs 3,580 gross profit per order at Rs 12,700 (Rs 3,380 at the Rs 12,500 listed price), a 27 to 28% margin. Under the simple model its break-even Meta cost per purchase is about **Rs 1,469 at 55% delivery, Rs 1,827 at 65% and Rs 2,185 at 75%**, so at Rs 800 per purchase it nets roughly Rs 669, Rs 1,027 or Rs 1,385 per order placed. The weak spot is returns: the earlier report's 38% printer return rate would put delivery near 62% and the net near Rs 900 per order. Ad-library saturation is low, with about 2 to 3 relevant Pakistani advertisers, none a classic gadget COD page, though the only PKR receipt-printer-type ad (a POS store) was a day old and the longest-running printer-type ad was about 50 days with the product unconfirmed ([MB Communication ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2597705934036756); [Deliverr.pk ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1363946649054174)). A thin field can mean an open niche or a niche that does not convert on Meta COD, and the library cannot tell which; the store's own 127 orders are the better evidence. No policy flag applies, but brand wording should avoid implying HP or Epson affiliation. Demo-ability is high: a receipt prints in seconds. Budget: keep Rs 1,000 per day as the protected base, and run the three angles below inside it rather than adding spend. Creative angle: a shop-owner "counter setup" demo (box to first receipt in 30 seconds), a price-plus-warranty card in Roman Urdu with the open-parcel line if the courier permits it, and a "bill printing kharcha" hook for a shop paying for handwritten bills. The 80mm roll (Rs 350 listed, Rs 200 cost, 42% margin, 136 non-cancelled units) is an add-on, not an ad lead; at Rs 150 profit it can only appear as a bundle line ("printer plus 5 rolls"). Owner check: confirm the Rs 200 delivery charge is collected on every order, and reconcile whether Rs 467 per booking or Rs 812 per purchase is the true Meta cost, because the earlier report found the two figures use different definitions.

**2. HP DeskJet 2320.** The DeskJet has the highest absolute margin of any proven item: **Rs 5,200 gross profit at Rs 20,200 (Rs 5,000 at the Rs 20,000 list price), 31 orders, 25 non-cancelled units, 23% cancelled**, slightly above the store average of 19.3% (Meit Shop Shopify data). Break-even cost per purchase is about **Rs 2,360 at 55% delivery, Rs 2,880 at 65% and Rs 3,400 at 75%**, so at Rs 800 it nets Rs 1,560 to Rs 2,600 per order placed, better than the SP-90A on the model. The Ad Library showed zero active ads for "HP DeskJet inkjet printer", which again means open space or no Meta-COD demand. The offsetting risks are a Rs 20,000 ticket, so each refusal strands Rs 15,000 of stock, and an inkjet that is heavier and more fragile to ship. Shopify shows total inventory 0 for the DeskJet despite continuing orders, so stock may be oversold or tracked elsewhere. Budget: Rs 1,000 per day, which is about 1.25 placed orders per day at Rs 800 per purchase. Creative angle: a "home and office print, scan, copy" demo showing all three functions on one device, aimed at students and home offices rather than shops, with a Roman Urdu price card. Owner check: physical stock count; confirm every order by phone before dispatch; verify the printer is a genuine HP unit and sold as such, since the trademark policy punishes clone hardware under a brand logo.

**6. Kisonli LP-2S (hold at maintenance).** The LP-2S has the second-highest order count (**84 units, 79 orders, 13% cancelled, Rs 0.27m non-cancelled**), but it sells at Rs 3,600, and its cost is not known. The earlier report fitted an assumed Rs 2,000 cost and found about Rs 55 net per booking, which is break-even; that cost is an assumption, not owner data. Under the simple model, clearing the Rs 2,000 gross profit floor at a Rs 3,600 price needs a **cost of Rs 1,600 or less**, and the Rs 2,800 preferred level would need Rs 800, which is implausible for a branded RGB speaker. The market is also working against it: Pakistani speaker advertisers are pushing Rs 1,250 to 1,300 products ([E-Bazaar ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2375639109934218)), and audio is the one electronics category Daraz named among its strongest 11.11 2025 interest areas, so price pressure will rise during the sale ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/04/darazmall-brands-lead-daraz-pakistans-11-11-sale-with-record-growth-and-customer-engagement-2/)). The sibling JEDEL SA148 (11 orders, 45% cancelled) is a warning about this category. Recommendation: keep existing ads at maintenance budget, no new test spend, until the owner confirms the cost. If cost is at or below Rs 1,600, move it up to rank 4 and test a bundle of speaker plus a second item to lift order value above Rs 5,000; if above Rs 2,000, stop advertising it and let organic orders continue.

## Seven bets: what each needs before a rupee is spent

For the bets, the decisive number is the ceiling on the wholesale cost. Applying the report's rule that gross profit must reach Rs 2,800 for a real return (Rs 2,000 is only the survival floor), the ceilings below use the Shopify listed price. They are computed, not quoted, and every one is a request to the owner for the actual Hafeez Center cost.

**3. BT600M portable 80mm printer.** This is the one bet with a known cost and it clears the gate: **Rs 3,096 gross profit (27.2% margin) at Rs 11,400**, with break-even cost per purchase of about **Rs 1,203 at 55% delivery, Rs 1,512 at 65% and Rs 1,822 at 75%**, netting Rs 403, Rs 712 or Rs 1,022 per order placed at Rs 800. Sales proof is thin: 3 units, 3 orders, 1 cancelled. The Ad Library found only a USD-billed page with no confirmed product, so the field is effectively untested rather than proven empty. Its audience differs from the SP-90A's, aimed at mobile vendors, delivery businesses and market-stall sellers who print on the move, so it widens the same buyer pool without cannibalising it. Budget: Rs 1,000 per day for 5 to 7 days after the DeskJet launch is stable. Creative angle: a vendor on the street printing a receipt from a phone, no counter needed. Owner check: stock level, Bluetooth app compatibility with Android and iPhone (pairing complaints are a likely return trigger, though unsourced), and warranty terms.

**4. Barcode scanners (Speed-X 60D, 9600, SP-9260, 8700).** Five orders across four models, four fulfilled and one cancelled, at Rs 8,000 to 10,000 (Meit Shop Shopify data). Cost is unknown for all. A Rs 10,000 scanner needs a **cost of Rs 7,200 or less** for Rs 2,800 gross profit (Rs 8,000 for the survival floor); a Rs 8,000 scanner needs Rs 5,200 or less. Price is the problem: Black Copper scanners are listed in Pakistan at Rs 6,000 to 7,150 at retail ([W11stop POS](https://w11stop.com/pos-systems)), well below the store's Rs 8,000 to 10,000, so unless the Speed-X models are 2D wireless units with a clear feature edge, buyers comparing on Daraz during 11.11 will undercut the store. The earlier report recommended scanners on the argument that the buyer is the same shop owner, but the sales data adds only five orders, which does not prove demand. Ad Library shows two PKR scanner advertisers, both new (2 and 4 days old), and no long-runner ([PosOye ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1641294590999980)). Budget: Rs 7,000 to 10,000 on one model, only after the cost check passes. Creative angle: a counter-top demo of scan, beep and billing line item, with "2D wireless" or "works with your POS software" as the differentiator. Owner check: cost per model, which one is the 2D wireless version, and Daraz price for the same model.

**5. SP-90A plus cash drawer bundle.** The drawers have sold three times (SP-320A twice, one cancelled; SP-340B once, fulfilled) at Rs 9,800 and Rs 10,900. A bundle of SP-90A (cost Rs 9,120) and SP-320A at about Rs 22,300 would carry the SP-90A's Rs 3,380 gross profit plus whatever the drawer adds, so it clears the Rs 2,800 bar as long as the bundle discount stays below the drawer's own margin. The drawer cost is unknown, and the Ad Library found zero relevant cash drawer ads (289 fuzzy hits, all foreign story apps), which is again either a gap or a signal of no demand. The risk is the Rs 22,000 parcel: a refusal strands about Rs 17,000 of stock, and the prior high-ticket argument for fewer refusals is untested (the printer's own return rate is 38%). Budget: Rs 1,000 per day for 5 days. Creative angle: "complete shop counter in one box," a time-lapse of unboxing and first sale. Owner check: drawer cost, whether the drawer connects to the SP-90A (RJ11 port) as sold, and what discount the bundle can absorb.

**7. Label printers (SP-700U and range).** One order, fulfilled, at Rs 19,500. The family runs from Rs 7,710 (SP-200U) to Rs 28,000 (Spt 710B), and prices are high enough that a **cost ceiling of about Rs 16,700 at Rs 19,500** clears Rs 2,800. This is a courier-and-seller product (Daraz and Instagram sellers printing shipping labels), a plausible fit with the Pakistani seller boom, but no source measured that demand. The Ad Library shows no label printer advertisers. It is a rank-7 test only after scanners and the bundle have run. Creative angle: a seller printing 20 parcel labels in a minute. Owner check: cost, and whether the buyer needs a different label roll supply (a repeat-sale add-on).

**8. Game sticks (X2 Pro 64GB, M22 64GB).** Four orders across two models at Rs 6,600 and Rs 7,110, one cancelled (Meit Shop Shopify data). Cost unknown; **the ceilings are about Rs 3,800 and Rs 4,300 respectively** for Rs 2,800 profit. It is demonstrable (plug into a TV, play) and fits a male-skewed Facebook audience, about 80% male in Pakistan ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-pakistan)), with wedding-season gifting a plausible but unsourced lift. No Ad Library search covered game consoles. A policy question the notes did not research: pre-loaded retro-game devices can trigger intellectual property flags, and "R36S console" style products already appear in Pakistani multi-product catalog ads, so check wording against Meta's counterfeit and trademark rules ([Meta Copyrights and Trademarks](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/intellectual-property-infringement/copyright-and-trademarks)); that risk is my inference. Budget: Rs 5,000 to 7,000, 2 concepts. Creative angle: the TV screen with the game already running, price card in Roman Urdu. Owner check: cost, whether the stick is branded or generic, real stock, and a return window.

**9. Cash counting machine (SP-640).** Two orders at Rs 22,500, one cancelled. Cost unknown; the **ceiling is about Rs 19,700**. This is B2B gear for shops, money changers and wedding-season cash handlers (the last unsourced). It demonstrates well (a stack of notes counted in seconds) and is shielded from commodity-price attacks, since the Daraz 11.11 category data skews to beauty, fashion and audio. The Ad Library did not show a relevant advertiser, and no demand source was found. The Rs 22,500 ticket makes confirmation calls non-negotiable. Budget: Rs 4,000 to 6,000 for a single-concept probe, after the earlier picks have been checked. Owner check: cost, counterfeit-note detection spec (a claim to avoid overstating), and warranty.

**10. IP wireless WiFi camera S06 (visible home security only).** Two orders at Rs 5,960, one cancelled; cost unknown; the **ceiling is about Rs 3,160**. WiFi cameras are the only category with a directional growth claim, from a seller blog without data ([Alif Dukan](https://alifdukan.com/blogs/topic/best-selling-gadgets-in-pakistan-2025-must-have-tech-products-for-every-home)), and the field is crowded with specialist security shops selling packages and installation (about 24 active ads, with IMOU and EZVIZ models at Rs 5,999 to 17,390), which implies a service-assisted sale that pure COD resale may not match ([Multi Solution ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=896599883446409)). Policy risk is real but manageable with careful wording: Meta's own page was not readable, and the only Meta-owned text found bars spy cams from Audience Network apps, while third-party guides say covert-use framing is restricted ([Meta Audience Network policy](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/audience-network/optimization/best-practices/an-policy)). Use "home and shop security" and never "spy," "hidden" or "secret." Rank 10, last, because it fits the weakest on cost, competition and policy.

## Products to skip and why

The store's own data and the economics rule out several items that look attractive in the catalog. **Earbuds** (JOYROOM EC06 at Rs 1,710 and M10 at Rs 1,000) fail on gross profit of Rs 560, negative break-even cost per purchase at every delivery rate, and the most crowded Pakistani Ad Library category, at least 12 PKR pages, mostly refreshing 11.11 ads and priced Rs 1,200 to 3,840; Daraz promotes earbuds at up to 81% off in the sale ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/11/11/the-best-11-11-earbuds-deals-with-up-to-81-discount/)). **Kisonli K12 speaker** has Rs 1,456 gross profit at Rs 6,400, below the Rs 2,000 floor. **HP M111W printer** earns Rs 5,000 to 5,500 in absolute terms but at a 13 to 14% margin on a Rs 39,000 ticket, leaving no cushion for any Daraz price undercut and Rs 34,000 stranded per refusal; the notes judged it not an ad candidate, and my reading is that the absolute-rupee gate passes but the risk profile does not. **Epson EcoTank L3250** (3 of 3 orders cancelled), **Dell and HP laptops** (Dell 15: 2 of 2 cancelled; Dell Vostro and HP OmniBook X cancelled) and the Rs 173,000 to 260,000 range fail on cancellations and stranded stock.

**JEDEL SA148 speaker** has 5 of 11 orders cancelled (45%) against a 19.3% store average. **Smartwatches** (X8 Ultra Max 10 units at Rs 2,540 ASP; REMAX Watch 9 with 2 of 3 cancelled) sit under Rs 3,000 or in a crowded field of 6 to 8 PKR advertisers, and the May 2026 FBR valuation ruling is expected to raise import costs ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/05/08/fbr-revises-customs-values-for-imported-smartwatches-bands-and-rings/)). **Power banks** (Romoss 30,000mAh, one order at Rs 4,530) face 8 to 9 PKR advertisers and claim-sensitive wording on battery capacity. **Mini and A9-style cameras** (Rs 1,230, two orders) are under the price floor and sit in the red policy zone. **Pixel4K X20 Plus TV box** (3 orders, 2 cancelled) has too small a sample, though it is a reserve candidate with a single Ad Library advertiser. **Phone stands and cables** have long-running ads but Rs 700-class retail prices that cannot cover an ad cost. Finally, projectors, recommended in the earlier report, do not appear in the store's sold items or catalog notes available here, so they cannot be named as a store product in this list.

## A two-week plan around 11.11 and 12.12

The plan assumes about Rs 1,000 per day per active slot, which is roughly 1.25 placed orders per day at Rs 800 per purchase, so one slot produces only 8 to 9 orders in 7 days, too few to read a delivery rate (the earlier report noted about plus or minus 17 points of noise at 30 bookings). Judge on confirmed orders and cost per confirmed order, not Meta-reported purchases, and treat every pass as permission for a second round. The 2026 Daraz sale dates were not confirmed; 2025 ran 10 to 21 Nov for 11.11 and 11 to 18 Dec for 12.12 ([CW Pakistan](https://cwpakistan.com/daraz-pakistan-11-11-sale-2025-offers-up-to-90-percent-off-and-digital-payment-incentives/); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/11/daraz-pakistan-closes-2025-with-12-12-grand-year-end-sale-and-one-last-wave-of-savings/)), so assume similar and verify. Pakistani 11.11 ads are already live, and no Pakistan Q4 CPM figure exists, so read your own Ads Manager history for the actual cost rise.

On days 1 to 2 (6 to 7 Nov) do the offline work: send the cost request below, count physical stock for the DeskJet and LP-2S, and set up a phone or WhatsApp confirmation step on every order of Rs 5,000 or more. On days 1 to 14 keep the SP-90A running at Rs 1,000 per day, because the B2B printer is the product least exposed to marketplace discounting during 11.11. From day 2 add the DeskJet at Rs 1,000 per day with 3 concepts (home print demo, student angle, office angle) and pause any concept that spends Rs 2,400 to Rs 4,000 (1.5 to 2.5 times the Rs 1,500 to 2,000 allowable cost per purchase) with no confirmed order. From day 4, if the BT600M stock is verified, run it as the third slot for 7 days. Hold scanners, the bundle, label printers, game sticks, the counting machine and the camera until the cost sheet is back, and release only one at a time into the slot that frees up. Around day 10 to 12 (during the sale), pause or cut low-margin products if the cost per purchase rises above the break-even figures in the table above. After the sale, from about day 12 to 14, read the first cancellation and delivery outcomes and prepare the 12.12 round, taking the best two bets from this test into it. Do not enter 12.12 with an untested product at full budget.

The owner must send, before each bet is released: the Hafeez Center cost per piece for each model (scanner models 60D, 9600, SP-9260 and 8700; SP-320A and SP-340B drawers; SP-700U and the label range; X2 Pro and M22 sticks; SP-640; S06 camera; LP-2S), the actual stock and exchange policy, warranty period, and the Daraz price for the same or a near-identical model. Without those, a bet stays at zero spend.

## Conclusion

The honest shape of this list is two proven products, one proven-but-unprofitable product, and seven bets, with the store's real edge being a niche where almost nobody advertises: business equipment for Pakistani shop owners. That edge is double-edged. The same lack of competing Ad Library advertisers that makes the SP-90A and DeskJet affordable to advertise could also mean most shopkeepers do not buy on Meta, and the 127 SP-90A orders are the only evidence against that. The cheaper path to a bigger catalog of winners is not more products but better economics on the winners: delivery rate moves break-even by about Rs 350 per ten points on the SP-90A, so confirmation calls and address checks may add more profit than any new product in the bet list.

The second takeaway is that the unknown cost prices, not the market, decide the order of the bets. Seven of ten picks cannot be called profitable or not until the owner sends costs, and for six of them the cost ceilings stated above are the test a Hafeez Center quote must pass. The day spent collecting quotes is the highest-value task before 11.11.
