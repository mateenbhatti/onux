## Which categories reach Rs 1,500-3,500 profit per delivered parcel at Rs 500 ad cost and 30-40% returns? Worked examples

### Takeaway
Back-solving the seller's own numbers gives an exact formula, and it shows Rs ~2,700 profit per delivered parcel is needed to net Rs 1,000 per booking, and ~Rs 3,500 to net Rs 1,500. Only items with roughly Rs 3,000+ gross margin after courier and tax qualify, which rules out sub-Rs 3,000 impulse gadgets.

### Cited Findings
- Seller-provided inputs (from the brief): Kisonli LP-2S retail Rs 3,600, cost Rs 2,000, profit Rs 1,148 per delivered parcel; SP-90A printer retail Rs 12,500, cost Rs 9,000, profit Rs 2,692; 38% of parcels return; return costs ~Rs 500.
- Courier fee: the brief says about Rs 350-600 per parcel; I found no published Leopards/Trax rate card. Leopards' pages describe COD service with no per-parcel fee — [Leopards COD](https://leopardscourier.com/services/cod)

### Computed (arithmetic, not sourced)
Model that reproduces the seller's two profit figures exactly: profit per delivered = (price + 200 shipping) - cost - Rs 500 courier - 4% x (price + 200).
- Kisonli: 3,800 - 2,000 - 500 - 152 = 1,148. SP-90A: 12,700 - 9,000 - 500 - 508 = 2,692. Both match.
Per booking = 0.62 x delivered profit - 0.38 x 500 return cost - 500 ad.
- Kisonli LP-2S: 0.62 x 1,148 = 712 - 190 - 500 = about Rs 22 per booking (break-even).
- SP-90A: 0.62 x 2,692 = 1,669 - 190 - 500 = about Rs 979 per booking.
Required delivered profit D for target booking profit P at 38% returns: D = (P + 690) / 0.62. P = 1,000 needs D ~ Rs 2,726; P = 1,500 needs D ~ Rs 3,532. At 30% returns: D = (P + 650)/0.70, so P = 1,000 needs D ~ Rs 2,357. At 40%: D = (P + 700)/0.60, so P = 1,000 needs D ~ Rs 2,833.
Gross margin needed (price + 200 - cost) for D = 2,726: about Rs 3,330 at a Rs 8,000 price (tax 328); about Rs 3,520 at Rs 12,000 price.
Illustrative only (ASSUMED costs, no wholesale quote found): BC-5890-class receipt printer, retail 8,270, assumed cost 6,200: D = 8,470 - 6,200 - 500 - 339 = 1,431, per booking = 887 - 190 - 500 = about Rs 197. A Rs 15,000 scanner at assumed cost 11,000: D = 15,200 - 11,000 - 500 - 608 = 3,092, per booking = 1,917 - 190 - 500 = about Rs 1,227. Treat as templates; replace costs with real quotes.

### Inferences
- Earbuds, cheap power banks, phone cases, LEDs, USB fans cannot reach D ~ 2,700: with price under Rs 2,100 the maximum gross margin is a fraction of that. They only work with bundles/upsells raising the order value.
- Candidates that can plausibly clear the bar are Rs 8,000+ items with ~30% gross margin: label printers, barcode scanners, receipt printers, air fryers (if cost margin 20%+ on Rs 20k+), mini projectors, dash cams. Supplier cost is the deciding unknown.
- Reducing return rate matters more than margin: at 30% vs 38% returns the same product gains about Rs 130-200 per booking.

### Gaps
- No real wholesale quotes, so no product can be confirmed to clear Rs 2,700; courier fee, whether the Rs 200 is paid by the customer, and the return cost (Rs 500) are assumptions from the brief.

## Which categories historically have lower COD refusal in Pakistan?

### Takeaway
No source found that breaks COD refusal down by category in Pakistan. The claim that B2B/high-ticket POS gear refuses less is plausible but unconfirmed, and one source argues the opposite for high cash-at-door amounts.

### Cited Findings
- Pakistan COD RTO estimates: 25-30% typical (Simpaisa, 23 Jun 2026); DHL puts it around 18%; EasySell says regularly above 40%; others say 30-40% for some categories. All are vendor blogs — [Simpaisa](https://www.simpaisa.com/blogs/why-cash-on-delivery-is-killing-your-e-commerce-business-and-what-to-do); [DHL Pakistan](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-pk/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-best-practice/subscription-marketing/how-pakistani-e-commerce-sellers-can-reduce-cod-returns); [EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/cod-ecommerce-pakistan-bangladesh-cash-collection-delivery-2026)
- COD is about 93.7% of Pakistani e-commerce transactions per one vendor blog; another cites over 85% — [EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/cod-ecommerce-pakistan-bangladesh-cash-collection-delivery-2026)
- EasySell says 70% of COD delivery failures in Pakistan/Bangladesh are operational (wrong address, unreachable phone, courier) rather than fraud — same link.
- A reseller guide states no audited national figure exists and that numbers circulating in groups belong to other people's products; it suggests the higher the cash required at the door, the more the door becomes a decision point (opposite of the B2B-is-safer thesis) — [CODVerifier on Hashnode](https://hashnode.com/@codverifier)
- India evidence (not Pakistan): electronics avoid size/fit returns, and higher-value buyers tend to be more intentional — [Base.com India RTO by category](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/?p=22444)

### Inferences
- Items bought for a business purpose (POS printers, scanners, label printers) are needed by a shop owner with a specific use, so intent is higher than for impulse gadgets; this is reasoning, not data. The seller's own 38% return rate on a Rs 12,500 printer and Rs 3,600 mic shows no evident advantage so far, so the seller should compare its own return rates by SKU.
- Phone-verification (WhatsApp/call confirmation) before dispatch addresses the operational 70%.

### Gaps
- Courier-level refusal data by category or order value (Leopards, Trax, TCS) was not found.

## Are there import, PTA, warranty or product-safety issues?

### Takeaway
Smartwatches face a 2026 customs valuation ruling; PTA type approval applies to radio devices and the position for Bluetooth-only items in 2026 could not be confirmed. Duty figures online conflict, so verify with a clearing agent.

### Cited Findings
- FBR revised customs values for imported smartwatches, smart bands and smart rings via Valuation Ruling No. 2076/2026 (Section 25A, Customs Act 1969) for non-GSM wearables from all origins, to curb under-invoicing. Actual values not found — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/?p=1015460)
- S.R.O. 1063(I)/2026 (30 Jun 2026) sets additional customs duty rates; the excerpt seen covered mainly food/agricultural lines, so electronics impact unconfirmed — [FBR](https://download1.fbr.gov.pk/SROs/20266301763307131063-2026.pdf)
- A 2019 PTA update is said to require type approval for devices such as Bluetooth speakers/peripherals; PTA tax per IMEI applies to cellular phones — [Bureau Veritas on PTA](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/pakistan-pta-update-list-exempted-device-type-approval-requirement)
- Third-party sites disagree on duty (10-20% vs 8.5% customs plus 18-20% sales tax; parcels under about PKR 10,000 duty-free claim) and are not authoritative — [Goods Across Borders](https://goodsacrossborders.com/importing/into/pakistan); [DutyDecoder](https://dutydecoder.com/calculate/china-to-pakistan-electronics-import/)

### Inferences
- Buying from local wholesalers (Hafeez Center etc.) moves the import and PTA burden to the supplier, but grey-market stock can have no warranty, which drives post-sale disputes. Heaters, blankets, chargers and power banks (lithium batteries) carry fire/safety risk and consumer complaints; no Pakistani standard (PSQCA) source was found.

### Gaps
- Actual smartwatch valuation figures; PTA status for TWS earbuds and wireless mics in 2026; PSQCA requirements for heaters, blankets, power banks; warranty norms. Not found.
