# Pakistani Meta advertiser activity and saturation by gadget category (Ad Library, snapshot 6 Nov 2026)

Method and limits (read first). The Meta Ad Library tool (mcp__Facebook_Ads__ads_library_search) worked, with country=PK and status=ACTIVE, 11 keyword searches run on 6 Nov 2026. It returns only: page name, ad link title, creation and delivery-start timestamps, billing currency and snapshot URL. It does NOT return ad body text, media format (video vs image), spend or impressions, and results are newest-first and keyword-fuzzy (a "power bank" search returned 3,848 hits, mostly foreign romance-novel ads; "cash drawer" returned 289 hits, none relevant). So "estimated_total_count" is not a product count, and I counted only ads whose title or page plainly matched the product. "Ad age" = days from ad_delivery_start_time (converted from unix time) to 6 Nov 2026; it shows the oldest start date the library gives, not proof of unbroken delivery. "PKR" currency is my proxy for a Pakistan-based advertiser; USD-billed pages that still reach PK are listed separately and not counted as Pakistani. Creative format and hook beyond the link title could not be seen, so format claims are gaps. Earlier work (SP-90A break-even, Rs 2,700 profit floor, scanner/projector idea) was not repeated; this note only adds ad-market evidence.

## Q1. How many active Pakistani advertisers/ads per category, how old, what hooks and prices?

### Takeaway
Cheap, impulse categories (earbuds, Bluetooth speakers, power banks, phone stands, chargers, smartwatches) have many PKR-billed pages launching new ads daily, mostly priced Rs 700-3,500, while the business-equipment categories (receipt printers, barcode scanners, cash drawers, inkjet) have almost no Pakistani gadget-style advertisers at all (0-3 pages each, nearly all under 5 days old). Mini cameras and TV boxes are thin but include one 166-day-old camera ad.

- The fact that no Rs 8,000+ POS gadget ad has run longer than 17 days (and only one is PKR-billed) suggests either an unserved niche or demand that does not convert via Meta COD; the library cannot distinguish them.
- Many ages are short because everyone is refreshing creatives for 11.11, so a short age this week is weak evidence of anything.

### Gaps

- Web search was not used because the tool worked; no agency or blog corroboration was gathered.

## Q2. Which categories look least crowded but still have long-running ads?

### Takeaway
Mini WiFi cameras are the clearest case (4 hits, one ad running about 166 days and another about 92), followed by WiFi-router backup power banks (2 pages, 42 and 74 days) and universal phone stands (several pages, 100-157 day ads, but cheap). Receipt printers, scanners, cash drawers, inkjet and TV boxes are the least crowded but show no long-running Pakistani ad, so they are open space of unproven demand.

### Inferences
- Mini cameras and router-backup power banks combine few advertisers with at least one 40-170 day run, the best fit for "uncrowded but proven". Mini cameras may be price-sensitive (A9 clones) and carry a privacy/policy risk for "spy" angles, which I did not verify.
- Phone stands show durability but sit around Rs 700 retail, which the earlier report says cannot cover a Rs 467-812 ad cost; they are proof of ad longevity, not a profitable target.
- Load-shedding relevance of the router backup hook is plausible given the Urdu wording but I have no demand data.

### Gaps
- "Running 166 days" may reflect an ad that was edited or relaunched and does not prove profit; spend and sales unknown.
- No ad-age evidence for barcode/POS beyond a week, so their lack of long-runners may be a sampling artifact (newest-first).

## Q3. Which hooks and angles recur in long-running Pakistani ads?

### Takeaway
The recurring pattern in older and newer PKR gadget ads is a concrete price in the headline, a trust assurance (free delivery, money-back or replacement warranty, open parcel before paying, "100% asli"), and a local problem hook (load shedding, battery anxiety, "TV price se kam" cinema). Roman Urdu phrasing ("Sirf Rs", "Jaye To Bhi", "Chalu Rahega") is common in the sharper ads.

### Cited Findings
- Price-in-headline: "Sirf Rs 1,300", "Only Rs 1,999" (Velora Finds PK baby bottle warmer), "Just Rs. 699" (Darvaza, 157 days), "Rs 3,250" (Shopvibehub), "Rs 11,999" (USA Stock House) ([E-Bazaar](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2375639109934218); [Darvaza](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1542749060793323); [Shopvibehub](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1747886179616872)).

No Fraud." (Xcessorieshub, ~17 days) ([Pak Desire](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1594671208730512); [Umer Shop](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1825328835297381); [Luxora](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1153288140990364); [Xcessorieshub](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1745874523186836)).

- Bundles: "120W Charger + 2 Cables", "Smart Watch + Earbuds", TWS earbuds + watch + flashlight in one catalog, Apple adapter + free cable ([Smarthomes](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1801385614347435); [Prime Deals](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2115447399107967)).
- Catalog/multi-product ads (page name plus many products in one ad) are common for mixed gadget stores such as Gadgets Point, Four Shines, Faah Cart, NoveltoMart, Hamzastore.pk, Vmart.

### Inferences
- The most durable pairing is a COD-trust line plus a concrete, local-pain hook rather than a spec list; this fits the earlier reports' emphasis on open-parcel and inspect-before-paying messaging, but the library cannot tell whether those ads are profitable.
- Visual format (UGC demo video vs static price card) probably matters but could not be observed, so I cannot say which format the long-runners use.

### Gaps
- Only link titles were visible; primary text, voiceover language, and format are unknown.
- No performance data ties any hook to sales; long run time is the only proxy and is confounded by budget, relaunches and ad edits.
- Several "long-running" examples (Pak Desire, Idealancy, Luxora) have product types I could not verify from titles.
